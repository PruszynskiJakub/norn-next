The norn Cookbook

How norn is meant to be run. You set it up once, keep one Herdr workspace open with the heartbeat and the cockpit, and let notifications call you back. You file issues, talk the spec through with the agent, answer what it asks and decide at the gates. norn starts the agents, runs the checks, merges the base in and opens the PR. Each recipe says what you see, what you press, and what norn does on its own.

The one rule. Don't go looking for work. When something needs you, a notification fires and the cockpit lists it at the top, under Needs you. Move to its row and press the key the footer offers: the cockpit takes you there, or runs the command for you. Typing norn … yourself is the fallback, not the routine.

00 Why norn, in two minutes

Read this, or read it aloud to the person you are showing norn to.

You give a story to a coding agent and switch to something else. Forty minutes later you look back. The agent has been waiting on a question in a pane you closed, or it died halfway, or it ran the same failing test three times, said it was done, and nothing checked.

norn delivers a story one bounded step at a time and keeps one inbox of everything that needs you. You label an issue. norn gives it a branch and a worktree and walks it through a workflow: refine the spec with you, plan, implement in slices, test, review, QA, open a draft PR. Agents do the steps. Plain code decides what comes next, notices when an agent asks, dies or stalls, and puts it in front of you with the one action that resolves it.

A good fit

One person, Claude Code in Herdr, GitHub or GitLab, a test suite you trust, stories big enough to plan. Several stories in parallel on one machine.

Not a fit yet

One-line fixes (the overhead is not worth it), repos without issues and PRs, teams that require linear history, two machines on one story (designed, not proven).

The heartbeatnorn watch --human
Ticks every 30 s: observes agents, git and the remote, decides, acts. One line per thing it did. Leave it running.
The cockpitnorn cockpit
Full screen, one key per action. Left: what needs you, then one row per story. Right: the selected story, with its slices, PR, what runs and its notes. It never decides; it runs the same verbs you would type.
Agent tabsgh-18/02 · implement
Opened by norn in auto mode without taking focus, or by you from the cockpit for attended steps. The sidebar shows who waits for you.
Notificationsherdr · os · ntfy
Each new item fires once: a Herdr toast at the desk, a desktop notification, a push to your phone when you are away.

02 The map

The default story workflow. Blue steps are agents, amber steps wait for you, grey steps norn runs itself. Dashed lines are counted loops back. In practice you meet only the amber steps and the two attended blue ones.

agent stepwaits for a personnorn runs it

Each slice runs the slice workflow on its own branch and worktree: implement → test → review → qa → integrate into the story branch. After you approve ready, you merge the PR, and norn cleans up when it sees the merge.

WordMeaning
storya labelled issue assigned to you: one spec folder, branch, worktree and draft PR, e.g. gh-18
slicea piece of a story with its own branch and worktree, merged back into the story, e.g. gh-18/02
unita story or a slice: what a step runs on, and what an inbox item names
stepa node of a workflow: agent, check (a command), git, gate (a person decides) or slices
attended stepan agent step you do together with the agent (refine, clarify, plan). Always started by you
outcomehow a step ended (done, pass, approve…); the workflow maps it to the next step
countera bound on a loop (test_loop: max 5). Past the max, the unit is stuck
tickone pass of the heartbeat: observe, decide, act. norn watch ticks every 30 s
modewho starts unattended agent steps: norn (auto, how this cookbook runs) or you (manual, the built-in default)
claimwhich checkout drives a story; a git ref renewed by each tick
receiptan immutable record of something that happened, written before its effect

No recipe matches. Try a cockpit key, a command name like unstick, or a word from the inbox.

Outside the cockpit: norn cockpit --next (Herdr action norn.next) jumps to the oldest agent waiting for you. norn cockpit --open splits a Herdr pane below and runs the cockpit there. norn cockpit --json prints the model.
