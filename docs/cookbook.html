The norn Cookbook

Short, tested recipes for the situations you will actually meet: your first story, a day of driving several agents, an agent that asks or dies, a main branch that moved, and the workflows you will want to shape. Each recipe says when to use it, what to type, what norn does on its own, and what to watch for.

The one rule. Run norn inbox and do what it says. Every item prints the exact command that resolves it. Most recipes below are that rule applied to one situation.

00 Why norn, in two minutes

Read this, or read it aloud to the person you are showing norn to.

You give a story to a coding agent and switch to something else. Forty minutes later you look back. The agent has been waiting on a question in a pane you closed, or it died halfway, or it ran the same failing test three times, said it was done, and nothing checked.

norn delivers a story one bounded step at a time and keeps one inbox of everything that needs you. You label an issue. norn gives it a branch and a worktree and walks it through a workflow: refine the spec with you, plan, implement in slices, test, review, QA, open a draft PR. Agents do the steps. Plain code decides what comes next, notices when an agent asks, dies or stalls, and tells you which command answers it.

Your turn (1) gh-21 refine: attended step — work with the agent · 2m $ norn open gh-21 Answer (1) gh-18/02 implement: Keep the old /export endpoint for API clients, or remove it? · 6m $ norn answer gh-18/02 "…" Approve (1) gh-17 approve-plan: Review the Plan section in story.md and the slice files… · 25m $ norn approve gh-17 Running (1) gh-18/01 review #1 (auto) · 3m $ norn trace gh-18/01

A good fit

One person, Claude Code, GitHub or GitLab, a test suite you trust, stories big enough to plan. Several stories in parallel on one machine.

Not a fit yet

One-line fixes (the overhead is not worth it), repos without issues and PRs, teams that require linear history, two machines on one story (designed, not proven).

01 The map

The default story workflow. Blue steps are agents, amber steps wait for you, grey steps norn runs itself. Dashed lines are counted loops back.

agent stepwaits for a personnorn runs it

Each slice runs the slice workflow on its own branch and worktree: implement → test → review → qa → integrate into the story branch. After you approve ready, you merge the PR, and norn cleans up when it sees the merge.

WordMeaning
storya labelled issue assigned to you: one spec folder, branch, worktree and draft PR, e.g. gh-18
slicea piece of a story with its own branch and worktree, merged back into the story, e.g. gh-18/02
unita story or a slice: what answer, trace, why and friends take
stepa node of a workflow: agent, check (a command), git, gate (a person decides) or slices
attended stepan agent step you do together with the agent (refine, clarify, plan). Always started by you
outcomehow a step ended (done, pass, approve…); the workflow maps it to the next step
countera bound on a loop (test_loop: max 5). Past the max, the unit is stuck
tickone pass of the heartbeat: observe, decide, act. norn watch ticks every 30 s
modewho starts unattended agent steps: you (manual, the default) or norn (auto)
claimwhich checkout drives a story; a git ref renewed by each tick
receiptan immutable record of something that happened, written before its effect
