# Limitations

What norn does not do yet, what has not been proven in real use, and the limits of its design. What was fixed is in the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md). Read this before relying on norn beyond the envelope below.

## Proven envelope

What the test tiers cover end to end, with real git, real processes and a scripted agent:
- One person, on **one machine**.
- The GitHub tracker and forge (over a scripted `gh`), or the local tracker with no forge.
- The local store; several stories in parallel on one base.
- The headless runner; manual or auto mode.
- Claude Code and pi as harnesses, held to the same recorded sessions.

The `refs` store shared by several clones is tested with clones on one machine only. Everything else below is either a known gap or unproven.

## Not built yet

- **A rerun moves one unit, and only within its round.** `norn run rerun` sends one unit (a slice included) to a step of the workflow it pinned. It never takes up a unit that ended done or is corrupt, never acts on a story whose change merged or whose item is closed, and never on a story another machine's claim holds. A slice taken up again does not take up its story: a story that ended stuck at its `slices` step needs its own rerun there. A story still in progress whose claim nobody holds stays held (`claim.none`): the rerun is recorded but waits until someone takes the claim with `norn story take`.
- **No generated-file resolution.** A base update whose only conflicts are in generated files (emitted schemas, a generated reference) goes to the workflow's agent resolve step like any other conflict; no config names a command that rewrites them instead.
- **A new round is the whole story's, and only before its change merged.** `norn story round` starts a story's next round with fresh counters. It never starts a round for a slice alone, never on a story whose change merged or whose item is closed, and not on a story another machine's claim holds. With `--force` it ends a round under way, but only once no run of any kind (agent, check, plugin step) is left on its record unstopped and every slice has ended. A slices step counts a story's slice units by id, not by round, so a round that can come to a `slices` step is refused while the story has slice units from an earlier round (`slices_from_earlier_round`): start it at a step no `slices` step follows (such as `--at qa`), or close the story and create a new one for new slices.
- **A branch update is a one-off merge of the base, with no resolver.** `norn story update-branch` updates only a story whose units all ended and whose change is open, never a slice's branch, and never while a round is under way. On a conflict it aborts and refuses with the paths; resolving takes a round with an update step. A base conflict that only the real merge finds, after the change's head was merged in first, leaves that merge on the local branch, unpushed. It needs a vcs that merges in a story's own worktree. The update writes its `branch.update` intent before the first merge and its `branch.updated` outcome after the push; an update cut short between them (a crash, a failed push) leaves the merge on the local branch, and possibly on the remote, with only the intent recorded, until the verb is run again and finishes it under that intent. A round started meanwhile leaves that intent unfinished for good. It records nothing for an update that changed nothing, and a vcs without a remote is recorded as pushed though there was nowhere to push to.
- **A takeover moves the claim and the record, not the old machine's work in progress.** `norn story take --force` moves a story's claim from another holder, live or expired. It does not stop that machine's agent: a run there keeps going until it ends (stop it there first with `norn run stop`), and once the claim moved its report is refused there (`claim_moved`) and changes nothing. A unit's committed work reaches the remote at every flush of a held story (its branch pushed to `refs/norn/heads/<branch>`, never a branch, so no CI runs), and a new holder's worktree starts from it; commits the old machine made after its last flush, and work it never committed, stay on it. Run data the old machine wrote and never published (a report recorded just before the claim moved) is not merged: its next tick drops that part for the remote's record, kept under `refs/norn/v2/dropped/runs/<story>/` in that clone, and says so among the tick's problems. A worktree the old machine still has is moved forward to the newer published head when the story comes back to it; one whose branch gained commits of its own meanwhile is refused (a `conflict` on each launch) until a person merges the published head into it. No command gives a claim up early; a tick gives it up when the story ends. A store without the `takeover` capability can take a free or expired claim but not a live one. If the takeover's record cannot be written after the claim moved, the claim is still yours, and the record lacks that takeover.
- **The claim holder is `user@` the machine's name.** It is derived once (from macOS's LocalHostName, else the hostname) and kept in the norn home's `holder.json`, so a hostname that follows the network never moves it; a claim taken under a name this home used before stays this machine's, and its next tick moves it to the holder. Two clones on one machine under one user, even with separate norn homes, claim as the same holder: each takes the other's claims as its own, and both act on the same story. Give each its own `holder` (`norn config set holder <name>`) when one person runs two clones of a repo on one machine.
- **A decision sent to another machine shows only once applied.** An answer or gate decision sent through the mailbox waits there until the holder's next tick; until the holder publishes the outcome, your clone still shows the story waiting, and nothing lists the letters a story's mailbox holds. A holder whose ticks stopped leaves it waiting: take the story with `norn story take --force`, and your next tick imports it. A decision on a step the holder has since left is refused on import like a stale one made on the holder.
- **On the `refs` store a decision or a report needs the remote.** `norn story answer`, `approve` and `reject` fetch the run data and read the claim from the remote before they record or send, and an agent's `norn done`, `ask` and `stuck` read the remote's claim to tell whether it moved, so with the remote unreachable they refuse ("report again"), even on the machine holding the story.
- **Every clone of a shared store runs the same norn version.** A clone on an older norn would read a receipt with a field or kind added since as failing validation and mark the unit corrupt, so the store keeps a version floor: each norn that acts raises it to its own release, and a norn below it refuses every command that acts (`version_too_old`, exit 3) while `status` and `story view` still work; `watch` says so once and stops ticking. Upgrade every clone sharing a `refs` store together: the first clone to act on a new release locks the older ones out until they upgrade. The floor rises when the command starts, so an acting command that then refuses (a mistyped story key) still raises it. The floor only rises, and nothing lowers it after a downgrade. A `watch` already in a tick when a newer clone raises the floor can still read that clone's receipts during that tick and show the unit corrupt until its next check pauses it. With the `refs` store the check costs each acting command one more `git ls-remote`, and offline the command acts and notes on stderr that the floor was not checked.
- **Criteria verdicts show only in `norn story view`, and only those short of pass.** At a gate, `norn story view` lists the last report's verdicts that did not pass, each with its repro, reason or how to check it. `norn status` lists no criterion, so a verdict a person must check (an `unverified` one with its `how`) is seen only in the story view; and no command shows the `pass` verdicts with the evidence the agent gave for them: the pull request body quotes them.
- **No notifications and no live view.** Nothing tells you when a story starts waiting for you: keep `norn status` in view.
- **Secrets in pushed run data.** With the `refs` store, receipts, check output and the story's documents are pushed to the remote unredacted. Keep secrets out of check output.
- **No GitLab plugin.** The bundled tracker and forge are GitHub's and the local ones; another host needs a plugin ([Recipes: Write a plugin](recipes.md#write-a-plugin)).
- **One pack per repository.** The config names one pack; a repo's own workflows in `.agents/workflows/` lay over it.
- **No permission profiles per step.** Claude Code runs in its `auto` permission mode by default. A permission prompt the agent is blocked on parks the unit, and `norn status` shows the call it asks about; `norn story answer` answers it only in a runner that can press keys in the agent's pane (herdr), and only on the machine that launched the run. A headless agent's prompt is answered nowhere but its own session: rerun the step (`norn run rerun`).
- **A trust prompt is not answered for you.** An agent tool that asks whether to trust a new worktree loads no hooks until a person answers, so its run sends no event. In a herdr pane, norn parks it on the next tick with the dialog's text, which herdr reads off the pane (any other pane runner: after the silent window), instead of retrying it; trust the folder in the pane, or `norn story answer <unit> yes`. norn never trusts a folder on its own.
- **The hooks are a reminder, not a sandbox.** In a unit worktree the harness's hook refuses a file-tool write outside the worktree or under norn's run state, and keeps the agent from ending a turn without a report (a few times a run, then the step's nudges take over), each with the rule's reason. It reads only file-tool writes, refuses no shell command, and lets anything but a guarded write through when it fails itself, so it is no security boundary. What it misses is still forbidden by the brief: shell writes under norn's run state, remote writes not on its list (it lists none: a `git push`, a `gh` call, a `curl`), and every norn command but `brief`, `done`, `ask` and `stuck`.
- **No guards on transitions** (`when:`), no per-slice mode, no `fork`/`join` steps: conditions are outcomes, a person's QA is a gate, and `attended: true` on a step covers the precise cases.
- **Cost per story** is not tracked.
- **No mode per story.** The mode is the repository's (`norn config set kernel.mode auto`) unless the story's workflow sets its own `mode:`, so a story created with a workflow whose mode is `manual` runs manual; no command switches one story between auto and manual as norn 0.35's `story edit --auto` and `--manual` did. The kernel still applies a mode change recorded on a story; nothing records one yet. To run one story's next step in manual mode's place, start it with `norn run start <unit>`.
- **A clean base update asks for the ready approval again.** norn 0.35 carried a person's `approve` at the ready gate over a base update that merged cleanly, when every check after it passed. This norn parks at the ready gate again after any run of the update step: approve it again with `norn story approve <key>`. The update step runs only under `base.stale: behind` or after a resolve; with the shipped `base: { stale: conflict }`, a base that moves cleanly under a story at ready or merge runs only the verify check, and the story keeps its approval.

## Dropped from 0.35

What norn 0.35 did that this norn leaves out on purpose, each with what to do instead. A 0.35 command is named here without `norn`, since it no longer runs.

- **No cap on agents at once.** norn 0.35 ran at most `max_agents` agent sessions on a machine (9 by default) and queued the rest. This norn starts every due agent and check in the tick that finds it due. A config that still sets `kernel.max_agents` loads, and `norn doctor` names it for deletion (`norn config set kernel.max_agents --unset`). To run fewer at once, keep fewer stories open, or set `kernel.mode: manual` and start each step with `norn run start <unit>`.
- **No port slots per worktree.** norn 0.35 gave each worktree its own port range (`.env.norn`, `PORT_BASE`) and stopped stray listeners; this norn does neither. An app a QA step starts picks free ports itself, and two worktrees running the same app at once can collide.
- **No story reset.** norn 0.35's `story reset` took a story back to before intake, deleting its record and its remote branches. Close it (`norn story close`), let a tick end it, remove its worktrees and local branches with `norn tidy`, and create a new story for the work.
- **Every person verb names its unit.** norn 0.35 took the unit from the worktree you stood in, or offered a picker, when you left the key out. A picker cannot run under `--json`, and a guess could decide on the wrong unit, so each verb now takes the key: `norn status` lists them.
- **No workflow change before the first step.** norn 0.35's `story edit --workflow` rewrote a story's workflow until its first step started. The workflow is read once, at intake, from `norn story create --workflow` or the item's workflow label; to change it later, start a new round with `norn story round <key> --workflow <name>`.
- **No `run list` or `run view`.** norn 0.35 listed a unit's steps as a timeline and traced each receipt. `norn story view <key>` shows a story's units, runs, gates and asks, and each tick's facts and decisions are in the trace under the machine dir (`trace-v2/`).
- **No `run attach` and no herdr sidebar labels.** norn 0.35 focused a unit's herdr pane and wrote each pane's status into herdr's sidebar. The herdr runner opens one workspace per story and one tab per run, each labelled, so you find a run in herdr itself; `norn status` says which unit waits for you.
- **No `done --evidence`.** norn 0.35 copied evidence files beside the receipt. Evidence goes in the criteria report instead (`norn done <outcome> --criteria <file>`, each `pass` with its evidence), where the PR body quotes it.
- **No `ask --option`.** norn 0.35 offered fixed choices with a question. An answer is free text: name the choices in the question itself, and `norn story answer` carries the reply.
- **No `--text`.** norn 0.35 accepted it and ignored it once markdown became the default. Every command prints markdown unless given `--json`.
- **No `upgrade`.** norn 0.35's `upgrade` rewrote a 0.30 repo's workflows and skills and stamped the version it ran. This norn writes the skills at each launch, in `norn init` and in `norn doctor`, and keeps the version floor in the store; to upgrade, install the new binary and run `norn doctor`.
- **No `uninstall`.** norn 0.35 removed the hooks and skills it had written into the checkout. This norn writes hooks only into unit worktrees, which `norn tidy` removes; to remove norn from a repo, delete `.agents/norn.yaml` and `.agents/norn.local.yaml`, norn's marked copies under `.agents/skills/`, the links and marked `.gitignore` in the harness's skills folder, and the `.agents/` or `.agents/norn.local.yaml` line `norn init` added to `.git/info/exclude` (left there, it keeps `.agents/` hidden from git). The labels `norn init`, `norn doctor` and `norn story create` defined on the tracker stay until you delete them there.
- **No REVIEW.md starter.** norn 0.35's init wrote a starter `REVIEW.md`. The review skill still reads one at the repo root when present: write it yourself, with the paths to skip and what counts as blocking.
- **No `status --details`, `status --notes` or `status --history`.** `norn status` now shows each waiting unit's question or prompt, and how many notes a gate has open, by default; the notes in full are in `norn story view <key>`, and the history of what needed a person is in the trace.
- **No `story view --web` and no `story start --print`.** norn 0.35 opened an item in the browser, and printed an agent's launch command for a person to run. Open the item with `gh issue view <n> --web` or `gh pr view <n> --web`; start a held step with `norn run start <unit>`, and the next tick launches it in the configured runner.
- **Taking a story no longer assigns its issue.** norn 0.35's `story take` assigned an issue carrying the intake label to you, so intake took it in; `norn story take` now moves a story's claim between machines. Assign the issue yourself (`gh issue edit <n> --add-assignee @me`) when `tracker.intake.assignee` asks for it.
- **No `story reopen`.** norn 0.35 undid a close, restoring the worktrees and claiming the story again. A closed story stays closed, and a round is refused on it: create a new story for the work.
- **No `sync`.** norn 0.35 synced the remote and ran intake on demand. Every tick does both: run one now with `norn tick`.
- **No commit trailers.** norn 0.35 put trailers on the commits it made, and named the story that landed on the base from them. This norn's own commits on a story branch are merge commits (a base update, a slice's integration, a fetched remote it cannot fast-forward), and each carries its meaning in its message, not in trailers; every other commit is the agent's, since `norn done` refuses a worktree with uncommitted work. A resolve step's brief names the base commit and the conflicting paths; to see which story landed on the base, read the PR's merge commit or `git log` on the base.
- **No notice when the issue body changes after refine.** norn 0.35 told you when an issue's body changed after its spec was written. Decide never reads prose, and the spec is the story's record once refine ends: carry a change into the spec yourself, or start a round at the refine step with `norn story round <key> --workflow <name> --at <step>`.
- **No attended reminder on every prompt.** norn 0.35's hook repeated an attended step's reminder on each prompt a person typed. The reminder now comes when the session starts and after the context is compacted, so it does not fill every turn; the agent reads the step again with `norn brief`.

## Not yet proven in real use

- **Two real machines.** Claims and the mailbox on the `refs` store are git refs with compare-and-swap, which is sound on paper and passes its contract suite, and a gate decision made on one clone reaches the clone holding the story through the mailbox in the integration tier, but no two real machines have shared a story.
- **The herdr runner.** CI drives it through a fake `herdr`; its tests against a real Herdr run only when asked (`NORN_TEST_HERDR=1`, with `NORN_TEST_HERDR_SESSION` naming the herdr session to use), never in CI, and no long dogfood run has used it yet.
- **Real agents in long runs.** The live canary runs one real turn of each agent tool when asked (`NORN_TEST_LIVE=1`); pi in particular has run few real stories, and reports no subagents and no usage limits, so norn cannot wait on either for it.
- **A browser criterion checked live.** Nobody has yet had an agent with a browser pass a criterion seen in a web page.

## Limits of the design

- **Agents report their own work.** A receipt records what the agent claimed. Gates and checks are only as strong as the commands you configure, and a weak check lets anything through.
- **Decide never reads prose.** Anything that lives only in a spec body, a handoff or a review comment does not influence the next step unless an agent turns it into a typed outcome.
- **Corrupt means stopped.** A record that fails validation marks the unit `corrupt`, by design. Hand edits to norn's run data block the unit until a person repairs it.
- **Trackers and forges hold no state.** Labels and comments norn writes on an issue are for people; norn reads its decisions only from the store. A story's workflow is read from its label once, at intake, and editing a label later never moves a story.
- **Merge, never rebase.** Story branches gain merge commits from the base. Teams that require linear history must squash on merge; the base then stays linear.
- **Coarse grain.** norn works per story and slice on a heartbeat. It is the wrong tool for quick interactive edits, and the overhead is not worth it for tiny changes.
- **Workflows are pinned per unit.** Editing a workflow does not affect units already in flight. This is intended, but surprising.
- **Nothing stops a person typing into an agent's pane.** With the herdr runner, an answer typed there bypasses norn's record: give decisions with `norn story answer` too.
- **The local environment must stay healthy.**
  - It needs git ≥ 2.38 (`norn doctor` checks it and `norn watch` refuses an older one), disk for one worktree per running unit, and the tools `norn doctor` names for the config: `gh` signed in for GitHub, the agent tool, `herdr` for the herdr runner. Check commands must do their own setup in a fresh worktree, such as `bun install --frozen-lockfile`.
  - Rate limits or a GitHub outage stop observation of the tracker and forge.
  - A sleeping laptop pauses every watch, and claims expire. They are taken again cleanly after wake, but nothing moves in the meantime.
  - Clean-up removes files only: the folders a finished story's logs lived in stay, empty. A unit that did not end done keeps its logs, as it keeps its worktree, and a session log a custom launcher wrote elsewhere stays too.
  - A finished story deletes a done unit's local branch only once every commit on it is somewhere else: its story's branch, the base, or the remote's copy of it. A story squash-merged whose remote branch was deleted and pruned keeps its local branch.
  - The remote's copy counts as the fetched remote-tracking ref. A local branch deleted because that copy held its commits loses them locally once the server branch is deleted and the next fetch prunes it; for merged work the base still holds them.
  - `norn tidy` removes a stuck unit's worktree even while a person has a pane or session open in it: close it first. A herdr pane norn opened for one of its runs is closed by tidy itself.
- **Observability covers only what goes through norn.** The trace records every tick's facts and decisions, and is kept for `trace.retain` (a week by default). Anything a person or agent does outside norn, such as pane keystrokes or hand merges, shows up only as facts observed later, without the reason.
